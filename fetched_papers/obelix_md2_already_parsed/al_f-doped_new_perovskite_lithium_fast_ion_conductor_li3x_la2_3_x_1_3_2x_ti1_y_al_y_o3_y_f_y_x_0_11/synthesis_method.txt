**1. Method Name:**
Solid State Reaction

**2. Procedure Summary:**
The materials were prepared by a conventional solid-state reaction method. Stoichiometric amounts of $\text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3$, $\text{La}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{TiO}_2$, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$, and LiF were used as precursors, with an additional 5% extra weight of $\text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3$ to compensate for lithium loss during high-temperature processing. All reagents were dried before use. The mixed powders were thoroughly ground in an agate mortar, then heated in platinum boats for decarbonation. After this initial heating, the samples were reground, pelletized, and covered with powder of the same composition to minimize further lithium loss. These pellets were then subjected to a final high-temperature firing to complete the reaction. For electrical conductivity measurements, the synthesized powders were pressed into discs in an evacuated die and subsequently reheated. These discs were then coated with silver paint, dried, and further heated to ensure good electrode contact.

**3. Key Conditions:**
*   **Decarbonation:** 700 °C for 4 hours.
*   **Main Reaction/Sintering:** 1,200 °C for 25 hours.
*   **Pellet Pressing:** 12 MPa in an evacuated die.
*   **Reheating for Electrical Measurements:** Approximately 1,050 °C for about 10 hours.
*   **Silver Electrode Application:** Dried at 100 °C, then heated at 600 °C for 30 minutes.
*   **Atmosphere:** Not explicitly stated for the main reaction, but typically air for this type of solid-state synthesis, especially given the lithium loss compensation.
